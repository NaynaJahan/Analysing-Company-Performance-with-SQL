The goal of this project is to use data taken from a Postgres database to conduct a thorough
analysis of the performance of the company in question. The dataset has 13 interrelated tables
that reflect numerous aspects of the company’s activities, including customers, staff, products,
orders, and suppliers. It is based on the well-known Northwind database, the fictitious company
that manages a large and intricate database with numerous stakeholders, including suppliers,
consumers, workers, and products, in several geographical regions. The organization must
overcome a few challenges to effectively analyze this data in order to boost business
performance. The main objective is to use SQL queries to provide answers to important business
queries that shed light on the company’s operations, sales figures, and customers by delivering
data-driven, actionable insights to corporate stakeholders that could guide decision-making.
Without advanced querying techniques and appropriate data organization, it might be
challenging to extract relevant insights from a large number of interconnected tables (Sarker,
2021). To ensure smooth operations and continued growth, it is essential to have the answers to
important business issues, such as identifying top-performing items, comprehending client
demographics, and tracking order fulfillment (Lemon & Verhoef, 2016).

After defining the foreign key and primary key relationships using SQL queries, the analysis
generated informative results for a range of departmental business purposes. In order to help the
Product Team evaluate offerings, a list of products with prices between $20 and $50 was
identified. This list classified products by price and showed those that were not discontinued.
Brazil and France were identified as major regions based on logistics performance in 1998, which
highlighted countries with more than 10 orders and shipping delays longer than five days. HR
insights aided in tracking career growth, including the employee’s age at employment and their
reporting structure. According to global logistics performance from 1997 to 1998, the best month
in terms of freight volume was April 1998. In order to help with pricing decisions, products with
notable price changes outside of the 20%–30% range were marked.

Key insights for sales volume and discounted totals were revealed by category performance
throughout price ranges, and regional supplier stock data assisted in determining the amount of
inventory required by region. Competitive pricing was supported by the comparison of product
prices to category averages, which showed which products were above or below their category
benchmarks. Employee sales KPIs showed Margaret Peacock and other top achievers with
specific performance measures. Ultimately, individual contributions within each area were
highlighted by employee sales across categories, which supported performance reviews and the
optimization of sales strategies.
